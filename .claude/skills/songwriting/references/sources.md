# Sources and evidence

- Ableton, [Arranging as a Subtractive Process](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/arranging-as-a-subtractive-process): fill the timeline, then sculpt by removal.
- Open Music Theory, [The Phrase, Archetypes, and Unique Forms](https://pressbooks.nebraska.edu/openmusictheory/chapter/phrase-archetypes-unique-forms/): phrase hierarchy, common lengths, sentence, continuation, and antecedent/consequent.
- Open Music Theory, [Introduction to Form in Popular Music](https://pressbooks.nebraska.edu/openmusictheory/chapter/intro-to-form-in-popular-music/): sections, formal function, boundary cues, and closing rhetoric.
- Berklee, [Jack Perricone interview](https://www.berklee.edu/berklee-today/summer-2018/Expert-Testimony): melody, groove, harmony, texture, prosody, and the “simple and interesting” principle.
- NPR Music, [Sampling Ain't Dead](https://www.npr.org/2020/04/30/848488734/sampling-aint-dead-hip-hop-producers-break-down-the-formula): producer perspectives from Just Blaze, 9th Wonder, DJ Dahi, DJ Premier, and Salaam Remi.
- Rolling Stone, [DJ Premier interview](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/dj-premier-interview-new-ep-1388437/): listening as a DJ, matching production to a voice, and sustaining a recognizable identity.
- NPR Music, [Pharrell Williams on juxtaposition and writing](https://www.npr.org/sections/therecord/2013/12/31/258406317/pharrell-williams-on-juxtaposition-and-seeing-sounds): idea, process, and contrast as a creator lens.
- Journal on the Art of Record Production, [Nile Rodgers: Navigating Production Space](https://www.arpjournal.com/asarpwp/nile-rodgers-navigating-production-space/): economy, strong rhythm-section foundations, breakdown/rebuild, and technology serving performance.

These sources support transferable methods. Artist names are reference lenses, not instructions to impersonate or reproduce protected songs.
