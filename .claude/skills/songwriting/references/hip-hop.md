# Hip-hop lens

Treat the rapper as the lead. The beat usually needs a foundation plus one strong identity: a loop, chop, riff, vocal fragment, or scratch phrase.

- Use subtraction for verses: remove a counterline, thin hats, or expose drums for a bar.
- Use `A A A A'` across eight bars. Let the last two bars answer or redirect rather than adding constant fills.
- Keep kick displacement legible against a clear backbeat. If every kick is syncopated, the listener loses the anchor.
- Make bass movement answer the melodic hook. Do not let both narrate continuously.
- Use scratches, vocal snippets, or a changed foreground phrase to define the hook without replacing the entire beat.
- Use conditional ghosts for flavor. Use a dedicated transition pattern for a required roll or drop.

Creator lenses: DJ Premier's DJ ear emphasizes matching sound to voice and keeping a recognizable signature. NPR's producer interviews treat sampling as selection, transformation, and context. Early expansive Kanye-style arrangements are a useful contrast lens: preserve the loop identity while adding section-scale orchestration and breakdowns. Do not claim or copy an artist's exact formula.
