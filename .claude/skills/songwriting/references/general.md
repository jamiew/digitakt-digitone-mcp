# General composition and arrangement

## Hierarchy

- Pattern: stored sequencer state.
- Pass: one playback of a pattern.
- Phrase: a complete musical thought, commonly 4, 8, or 16 bars.
- Section: phrases with one function, such as verse, hook, build, breakdown, or drop.
- Song: ordered sections and transitions.

## Roles and density

Assign foundation, harmony, foreground, response, and texture. Keep one busy foreground voice at a time. When one part moves, another holds or rests. Separate parts by time, register, rhythm, and timbre before fixing clashes with EQ.

## Phrase design

Use `call → response → repeat → turn` as a practical four-part phrase. A strong turn increases activity, removes weight, or points pitch toward the next downbeat. A continuation can use shorter fragments, faster rhythm, sequence, or faster harmonic change.

## Arrangement method

Build the fullest useful cell, duplicate it across the timeline, then subtract. Mark section boundaries with at least two changes, such as a fill plus a mute or a register lift plus added vocals. Review transitions in context, not solo.

## Test

The arrangement passes when the identity survives every section, each section has one job, foreground parts take turns, transitions land, automation resets, and the full sequence runs without manual rescue.
